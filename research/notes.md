## Customer and pain
Primary practical buyer is a civic hobbyist writer or small local newsroom maintaining issue continuity, not a generic resident. Secondary higher-value buyers are advocacy groups, developers, and zoning committees. The painful status quo is six meetings a week, scattered VODs/agendas/minutes, and manual reconstruction of decisions that unfold over years. Residents see search results but not an accountable answer to “what happened to this issue?”

## Gap, wedge, and timing
The wedge is a dated, sourced issue narrative maintained across years—continuity and current status rather than transcript search. Timing rests on two curves: 213 U.S. counties reportedly have no local news outlet and 136 papers closed in 2025, while transcription costs fell to roughly $0.04–$0.36/hour. The report cites about 50M Americans with limited/no local reporting.

## Competitors
Citizen Portal AI is the direct incumbent: free browsing, $15/month Pro, reported 2M meetings cataloged and 625K hours on pace for 2026. OpenPublica offers free transcript/search/alerts; OpenCity Record tracks locations; citymeetings.nyc is a solo summary product; Council Data Project is open-source infrastructure. Granicus, Zencity, and Polimorphic own government-side pipes. All could add timeline views, so editorial trust and local distribution—not extraction code—must compound.

## Demand signals and caveats
The report cites six-plus solo city trackers, a Lafayette hobbyist attending six meetings weekly for about 1,000 Substack readers, resident complaints about a 5.5-year Memphis speed-hump process and unfulfilled Stockton 2018 pledges, and a councilmember saying even council cannot track scheduling. Evidence proves pain, not willingness to pay. Google Ads demand was zero for the named monitoring terms. Only 543 neighborhood/block associations reportedly file Form 990, weakening the original association-buyer thesis. The 373K community-association universe mostly represents HOAs, a different segment.

## Offer ladder and GTM
- Free: one open timeline, updated weekly.
- Reader: $8/month or $80/year.
- Operator: $49/month per city for five timelines, embeds, sources, and change digests.
- Agency: $500–$1,500/month for custom tracking plus analyst notes.
- Source year-one assumptions: 2,000 signups, 15% reader conversion (300 × $8), two operators, one $500 agency buyer, about $3K MRR.

GTM is manual: one city, six issues, Reddit/local Substack comments, and direct outreach. No second city until one operator pays and readers repeatedly use the timelines.

## Biggest risks
Reader conversion benchmarks come from trusted human newsletters and may not transfer to SaaS. The named association buyer is sparse. Incumbents can copy the feature. Editorial verification, defamation/privacy, changed URLs, incomplete records, and automation hallucinations create trust risk. Scaling beyond roughly 20 cities becomes a people/content operation.

## Derived product choice
CivicThread prototypes the narrow operator workflow: inspect a sourced timeline, follow it, filter issues, and add evidence into an explicitly unverified human-review queue. It does not pretend AI extraction or alerts are live.
